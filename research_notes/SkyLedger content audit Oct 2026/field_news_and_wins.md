## Key Question 1: International governance (UNEA-7, London Convention/Protocol, EU/UK/Swiss/AU/national bans, Mexico, UNGA/IPCC)

### Takeaway
No new binding international ban emerged in 2026, but April–October 2026 produced the strongest-ever soft-law signals against deployment: the EU Council formally called for a moratorium on SRM deployment (21 Apr 2026), UNEP published a working paper titled "Scientific evidence does not support SRM as a viable climate solution" (12 May 2026), the G7 science academies suggested a moratorium on Arctic geoengineering (20 May 2026), India's NITI Aayog became the first Asian government body to announce plans to regulate solar geoengineering (June 2026), and the London Protocol parties adopted new assessment frameworks for ocean alkalinity enhancement and biomass sinking (9 Oct 2026). SRM was not on UNEA-7's formal agenda at all, and Mexico's 2023 "ban" still appears to be an administrative announcement rather than enacted law.

### Cited Findings

**UNEA-7 (Nairobi, 8–12 Dec 2025)**
- SRM was not on the formal agenda at UNEA-7; no draft resolution was tabled and no plenary decision was taken. It arose only informally in side events and hallway discussions. UNEA-7 was also marked by a formal US disassociation and failure to adopt the GEO-7 Summary for Policymakers — [DSG, 23 Dec 2025](https://www.sgdeliberation.org/dsg-writing/unea-7-and-the-quiet-conversation-on-srm)
- Ahead of UNEA-7, African ministers at AMCEN-20 reiterated that SRM technologies "pose significant and uncertain environmental, ethical and geopolitical risks, and must not be considered as viable options within the multilateral environmental agenda" — [ETC Group [advocacy]](https://www.etcgroup.org/content/african-leadership-rejects-solar-geoengineering-amcen20)
- EU Commission summary of UNEA-7 outcomes (15 Dec 2025) does not list an SRM decision — [European Commission](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/unea-7-conclusion-shows-multilateralism-still-delivers-2025-12-15_en)

**EU Council conclusions (21 Apr 2026)**
- Council document 8417/26 (21 April 2026): "The Council is concerned that large-scale climate interventions, in particular solar radiation modification (SRM), pose significant risks for the climate, the environment, security, and geopolitics. The Council calls for the full application of the precautionary principle to geoengineering, a close monitoring of initiatives, and a moratorium on deployment of SRM technologies," consistent with CBD and London Convention decisions; the EU "will continue to engage in discussions on potential international governance arrangements, including research related aspects" — [Council of the EU, 8417/26](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-8417-2026-INIT/en/pdf)
- Climate Home News reported this as the first time ministers of all 27 EU member states agreed such language; the statement was welcomed by both pro-research and non-use camps — [Climate Home News, 8 May 2026](https://www.climatechangenews.com/2026/05/08/eu-warns-on-solar-geoengineering-but-research-debate-grinds-on/)
- CIEL welcomed the precautionary emphasis but argued the EU must "align its policies with non-use" — [CIEL, 26 May 2026 [advocacy]](https://www.ciel.org/solar-geoengineering-developments-spring-2026/)
- The Council stance follows the December 2024 advice of the EU Chief Scientific Advisors and European Group on Ethics recommending an EU-wide moratorium — [Scientific Advice Mechanism](https://scientificadvice.eu/advice/solar-radiation-modification/); [European Commission, 9 Dec 2024](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/news/all-research-and-innovation-news/solar-radiation-modification-technologies-cannot-fully-address-climate-change-and-responsible-2024-12-09_en)
- The EU has issued a €35 million (~US$39.4m) tender for research on detection, monitoring, modelling and attribution of SRM activities (2026 call) — [Climate Home News, 8 May 2026](https://www.climatechangenews.com/2026/05/08/eu-warns-on-solar-geoengineering-but-research-debate-grinds-on/); [Cyprus funding portal listing of the 2026 call](https://www.fundingprogrammesportal.gov.cy/en/call/detection-monitoring-modelling-and-attribution-capabilities-for-solar-radiation-modification-srm-activities-and-assessing-their-impacts-on-earths-climate-and-ecosystems-en-2026/)

**UNEP working paper (12 May 2026)**
- UNEP published "Solar Radiation Modification: Scientific evidence does not support SRM as a viable climate solution – Working Paper" on 12 May 2026. It says current science does not back SRM as a response to climate change, cites major knowledge gaps, no global governance, and high uncertainty, and urges caution and transparency; it draws on a May 2025 UNEP–WMO workshop and a September 2025 multistakeholder meeting — [UNEP working paper](https://wedocs.unep.org/items/4154b06d-6a7a-4df0-aaa9-7472e4f7c6f5); companion Issue Note "No Substitute for Real Climate Action" — [UNEP](https://wedocs.unep.org/items/23bababf-e35a-4d98-8a01-388ad615c7f3)
- Critics on both sides: non-use advocates say the paper still leaves room for future use and outdoor experiments — [Solar Geoengineering Non-Use Agreement [advocacy]](https://www.solargeoeng.org/precaution-means-non-use-a-response-to-uneps-working-paper-on-solar-radiation-modification/); CIEL objects that the paper characterizes restrictive-governance advocates as wishing to "silence discourse" — [CIEL [advocacy]](https://www.ciel.org/solar-geoengineering-developments-spring-2026/); a cited researcher says the subtitle conflates "not viable" with "evidence does not support viability" — [SRM360](https://srm360.org/news-reaction/unep-report-draws-mixed-reactions-from-experts/)

**G7 science academies (Science7) Arctic statement (20 May 2026)**
- The S7 academies released three 2026 statements, including "The Global Arctic," presented to President Macron ahead of the G7 summit (Évian, 15–17 June 2026) — [Royal Society, 20 May 2026](https://royalsociety.org/news/2026/05/s7-statements/); [InterAcademy Partnership](https://www.interacademies.org/news/s7-academies-release-new-statements-brain-health-satellite-constellations-and-arctic-change); [Royal Society of Canada](https://rsc-src.ca/en/news/g7-science-academies-present-joint-statements)

**London Convention / London Protocol (7–9 Oct 2026)**
- LC 48/LP 21 met 7–9 October 2026. Parties adopted an updated ocean fertilization assessment framework and new frameworks for ocean alkalinity enhancement (OAE) and ocean sinking of biomass. The updated framework requires lab/mesocosm study before in-ocean activities, expands stakeholder consultation, and clarifies rules on economic interests (carbon-credit funding of research was contested). Some parties considered expanding the 2013 amendment to OAE premature until it enters into force — [Columbia Climate Law Blog, 7 Oct 2026](https://blogs.law.columbia.edu/climatechange/2026/10/07/parties-to-the-london-convention-and-protocol-debate-marine-carbon-dioxide-removal-governance/)
- The 2013 marine geoengineering amendment still is not in force: only nine countries have accepted (Australia, Croatia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Germany, Netherlands, Norway, UK); 37 of 55 Parties are needed — [IEAGHG summary of 2025 meeting](https://ieaghg.org/news/london-convention-and-london-protocol-annual-meeting-2025/); [OSPAR 2026 feeder report](https://oap.ospar.org/en/ospar-assessments/intermediate-assessments/intermediate-assessment-2029/non-indicator-assessment/feeder-report-2026-marine-geoengineering/); [IMO LC 47/LP 20 summary](https://www.imo.org/en/MediaCentre/MeetingSummaries/Pages/LC-47-LP-20.aspx)

**India (June 2026)**

**Latin America (June 2026)**

The declaration does not ban SRM; it urges caution and says decisions should rest on "scientific knowledge and multilateral governance," tasks the Environment and Tourism Commission with expert forums and a proposed Regional Framework Law on Climate Change Governance with geoengineering provisions — [Legal Planet, 16 Jun 2026](https://legal-planet.org/2026/06/16/legislating-sunlight-reflection-in-latin-america-and-the-caribbean/); [Degrees Initiative](https://degrees.ngo/science-policy-dialogue-at-parlatino-anchors-latin-american-srm-discussions-in-regional-evidence/)

### Inferences
- The combined EU Council moratorium call, UNEP's "not viable" paper, and the S7 Arctic language make 2026 the year the mainstream institutional center of gravity shifted toward "deployment moratorium plus research" — short of the non-use agreement advocates seek, but citable by SkyLedger as mainstream (not fringe) backing for restraint.
- SRM's absence from UNEA-7's formal agenda means the next multilateral decision point is likely UNEA-8 (2027) or the CBD; readers should not expect a UN ban in the near term.
- The London Protocol's October decision is a modest tightening (lab-first, stakeholder consultation, conflict-of-interest safeguards) but remains non-binding guidance while the 2013 amendment languishes at 9/37 ratifications.

## Key Question 2: Outdoor experiments in 2026 (ARIA, US universities, Australia, China/India/UAE cloud seeding, halted experiments)

### Takeaway
As of ARIA's 10 April 2026 update, the only ARIA-funded outdoor experiment that has actually run is the Cambridge-led Arctic sea-ice re-thickening (Real Ice) in Cambridge Bay, Nunavut, which reported up to ~50 cm thicker treated ice by May 2026; no UK outdoor SRM sites have been confirmed and UK marine cloud brightening (REFLECT) is not scheduled before 2028. The University of Washington's MCB program remains halted since Alameda (2024) with no 2026 field test found. The most consequential 2026 "experiment" controversy was not SRM but Rainmaker's unannounced 23 August 2026 drone cloud-seeding test near Homer, Alaska, which triggered a local backlash and a borough ordinance (see Q6). UAE ran 110+ seeding missions by August 2026; Delhi's trials returned "mixed results."

## Key Question 3: Major peer-reviewed findings in 2026 and scientific-body statements

### Takeaway
2026 produced no single landmark study but a steady stream: covert SAI would likely be detectable (Smith et al.), SAI could substantially alter the Indian monsoon (Wilson & Bala), high-latitude SAI would expose airline passengers to sulfate (Robock et al.), and GAO reiterated cloud-seeding gains of 0–20% that are hard to verify. The only formal scientific-body statement found was the G7 academies' hedged Arctic moratorium suggestion; no NASEM, AGU, AMS, Royal Society (standalone), or EGU 2026 statement on SRM was found. A prominent Guardian intervention by four senior scientists on termination shock (June 2026) is the most citable "expert warning."

### Cited Findings
- **Covert SAI detectability:** Smith, Alberola, Boers et al. (2026) find large-scale covert SAI would likely be detected by satellite and aircraft monitoring — [Environmental Research: Climate](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2752-5295/ae7148)
- **Indian monsoon:** Wilson & Bala (2026): SAI could substantially alter the Indian summer monsoon — [Environmental Research: Climate](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2752-5295/ae7599); ABC reports Indian Institute of Science research indicating significant effects on monsoon timing, amount, duration — [ABC News, 23 Jun 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-06-23/solar-geoengineering-srm-private-companies-monsoon-india/106805998)
- **Airline passenger sulfate exposure:** Robock, Duffey et al. (2026, GRL) on sulfate exposure for passengers under high-latitude SAI — [GRL](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2026GL122804); coverage — [New Scientist](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2532757-geoengineering-could-expose-plane-passengers-to-sulphuric-acid/)
- **Africa drying:** Egbebiyi, Fafure et al. preprint on SAI and drying over Africa — [EGUsphere 2026](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-3436/)
- **Air quality/mortality:** Wang et al. 2026 (NASA GISS / ACP): air-quality impacts of SAI are small at 1.5 °C, mainly climate-driven — [NASA GISS abstract](https://www.giss.nasa.gov/pubs/abs/wa01010x.html)
- **Ozone/middle atmosphere:** CCMI-2022 SAI scenario: five models agree on location of ozone-relevant changes but disagree on magnitude — [ACP 2026](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/26/11207/2026/)
- **Amazon/land carbon (modelled benefit):** SAI could increase land carbon storage and protect the Amazon — [Earth System Dynamics 2026](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/17/387/2026/)
- **AMOC/overshoot (preprint):** aerosol deployment location affects the AMOC with long-term uneven changes — [EGUsphere 2026](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-5188/)
- **Hunga Tonga eruption produced net cooling:** Quaglia, Visioni, Bednarz et al. — [ACP 2026](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/26/7677/2026/)
- **Sulfur precursors:** Franke, Smith et al. compare six sulfur precursors (incl. H2S, SO3) — [ES&T 2026](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.5c15172)
- **Ship-emission "inadvertent termination" analogue (2024, still widely cited):** 2020 IMO sulfur cut (~80%) produced measurable warming — [Comms Earth & Env 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-024-01442-3)
- **Termination shock explainer (13 May 2026)** citing a 2026 Möller & Young paper on abrupt suspension in GeoMIP G2 — [SRM360](https://srm360.org/article/what-is-termination-shock/)
- **Phaseout emulator study (preprint):** no post-phaseout warming trend over 20 years under deep CDR scenarios; author notes this does not establish real-world safety — [Research Square, Oct 2026](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-11247049/v1)
