# Stardust Solutions — developments April 2026 to October 26, 2026 (content audit)

Research date: 2026-10-26. Scope: what is new since April 2026 and whether SkyLedger's current Stardust profile is outdated.

Confidence labels used below:
- **[PRIMARY]** = checked directly against a primary record (Senate LDA database API, WIPO/PCT publication on Google Patents, arXiv/EGUsphere metadata, company website).
- **[MULTI]** = reported consistently by two or more independent outlets.
- **[SINGLE]** = reported by one outlet only.
- **[RUMOR]** = advocacy/Substack/forum claim, not corroborated.

Name note: the CEO's name is rendered "Yanai Yedvab" in NYT/E&E/CNN/Politico and the patent records ("Yanai YEDVAB"); Geoengineering Monitor uses "Yadov" and CIEL once wrote "Yedbab". Use "Yedvab".

Domain note: stardust-initiative.com now returns a 301 redirect to https://www.stardustsrt.com/ (checked 2026-10-26). SkyLedger's profile should update the company URL.

## Key Question 2: Experiments — what flew, where, when, what particle, altitude, regulator, response; further flights after April 2026?

### Takeaway
No in-aircraft stratospheric experiment took place in April 2026 or at any point since, according to every outlet that asked (NYT May 14, CIEL May 26, ABC June 23, Latitude July 1, Haaretz July 2, CNN Sept 8). Stardust told the NYT it "no longer intends to proceed on that timescale" and would run outdoor trials only with a government setting the rules; CNN reports outdoor tests are hoped for "in the next few years". SkyLedger's statement that Stardust "began in-aircraft stratospheric experiments April 2026" is contradicted by the record and must be corrected.

## Key Question 3: Patents — has any Stardust patent published? What does it disclose?

### Takeaway
Yes. Three PCT (WIPO) applications assigned to Stardust Labs Ltd published between May 21 and July 2, 2026, covering the particle, the monitoring/batch-tagging method, and the airborne dispersal system. The particle claim names amorphous silica and/or calcium carbonate at 0.1-2.0 micron diameter. SkyLedger's "particle composition secret, patent pending" is outdated.

### Cited Findings

Inventors: Tzemah Kislev, David Avnir, Alina Berkman, Ido Cooperstein, Yoav Lederer, Shlomo Magdassi, Karine Neimann, Gal Schwartz, Yair Segev, Amyad Spector, Eli Waxman, Yanai Yedvab. Claim 1: "A plurality of particles, each ... comprising: at least one of amorphous silica and calcium carbonate; and a particle diameter in the range of 0.1 um to 2.0 um; ... configured for dispersal in the stratosphere for solar radiation modification; ... safe for inhalation and ... safe for the environment; ... inert to a number of reactive compounds found in the stratosphere". ### Gaps
- No granted patent anywhere; no US national-phase publication found. No search of Israel Patent Office was possible.
- Whether Stardust has committed to any license-to-governments or open-IP policy for these filings is unreported.

## Key Question 5: Governance — code of conduct, transparency, advisory board, scientist criticism, Non-Use Agreement response

### Takeaway
Stardust published its long-promised guiding principles and a 14-page safety/controllability framework on April 2, 2026 (16-19 months after Pasztor's recommendations), disclosed its particle and posted eight preprints on May 14, 2026, and says it is "convening an advisory board" with Global South voices (June 2026, no names yet). Critics (Talati, Visioni, Safford) say self-governance by a for-profit "does not work"; Stardust declined to commit to pre-registration or raw-data release.
