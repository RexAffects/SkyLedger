# SkyLedger's Learn Pages Need Thirty Corrections Before January

*Content audit of skyledger.org/learn as of October 26, 2026. Built from five research notes (state legislation, federal action, field news, funders and startups, Stardust Solutions) checked against the live page source in `src/app/learn/` and `src/lib/data/`. Notes on source quality: "single-source" and "sources disagree" flags appear inline.*

The site's two loudest claims are now wrong: Stardust Solutions did **not** begin stratospheric experiments in April 2026 (every outlet that asked, from the NYT in May to CNN in September, reports lab-only testing and an abandoned timeline), and the particle the site calls "secret" was disclosed on May 14, 2026 and published in three WIPO patent applications between May 21 and July 2 ([CIEL](https://www.ciel.org/solar-geoengineering-developments-spring-2026/); [Google Patents WO2026105114A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2026105114A1/en)). Every bill on the Pending Bills page is dead or superseded except Pennsylvania SB 508, which has had no action since March 2025; Iowa, which the site marks "advancing," died at the legislature's second funnel after passing one chamber ([The Gazette](https://www.thegazette.com/news/state/which-bills-passed-the-iowa-legislature-this-year-which-didn-t/article_689bc91e-6bc9-48aa-9c4e-e7ef0b416184.html)). The enacted-ban count of three (TN, FL, LA) still holds, but two of the three penalty lines are unsupported by the statutes: Tennessee's act has no penalty section and Louisiana's official digest lists none, contradicting the site's "$10,000/day" and "$200,000 per violation" ([TN SA0653](https://www.capitol.tn.gov/Bills/113/Amend/SA0653.pdf); [LA Act 95 digest](https://legis.la.gov/LEGIS/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1426146)). Since April the field has moved in ways readers should hear about: Louisiana amended its ban (Act 601, effective Aug 1, 2026), Arizona's governor vetoed a passed ban, a Kenai Peninsula Borough ordinance became the first local permit regime written in direct response to a Rainmaker operation, the EU Council called for a deployment moratorium, UNEP said the evidence does not support SRM as viable, Rainmaker raised $100M, and the Clear Skies Act lost its sponsor when Greene resigned ([LA SB 189](https://legis.la.gov/legis/BillInfo.aspx?s=26RS&b=SB189&sbi=y); [Gov. Hobbs](https://azgovernor.gov/office-arizona-governor/news/2026/03/governor-katie-hobbs-legislative-action-update-0); [Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/news/environment/2026-10-22/kenai-peninsula-borough-adopts-new-rules-for-weather-modification); [Council of the EU 8417/26](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-8417-2026-INIT/en/pdf); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/rainmaker-raises-100m-series-b-to-scale-low-cost-water-production-and-weather-modification-302884251.html)). For Arkansas, no bill, sponsor, or interim study has ever surfaced; pre-filing opens November 16, 2026 and the session convenes January 11, 2027, so the state would be a first-time filer with three enacted models and one instructive veto to learn from ([Arkansas Legislature 2027 dates](https://www.arkleg.state.ar.us/Home/FTPDocument?path=%2Fassembly%2F2027%2FDocuments%2FSession_Important_Dates_2027.pdf)).

## Kenai's ordinance and Arizona's veto bracket a year of state churn

The single most useful new story for SkyLedger's readers is not a statehouse at all. On **August 23, 2026 Rainmaker flew an unannounced drone cloud-seeding test near Homer, Alaska**, releasing roughly 375 grams of silver iodide into a supercooled cloud and later claiming ~19 million gallons of added water. Alaska's Department of Natural Resources said it could not permit the operation because state law does not address cloud seeding, and the Department of Environmental Conservation found no evidence of harm — which is precisely the problem the episode exposed ([Alaska Public Media, Sept 10](https://alaskapublic.org/news/2026-09-10/a-california-based-company-made-it-rain-near-homer-locals-say-they-were-blindsided); [Sept 17](https://alaskapublic.org/news/environment/2026-09-17/after-rain-experiment-near-homer-borough-assembly-seeks-regulations)). Within two months the Kenai Peninsula Borough Assembly passed a resolution asking the Legislature to ban or regulate weather modification, and then on **October 20, 2026 adopted an ordinance, effective immediately**, requiring a commercial lease or land-use permit for any weather modification on borough land, with applications that must disclose the type and quantity of chemicals and flares, flight frequency and duration, and intended outcome, plus an outright ban on land designated for casual use. The sponsors were Republican Mayor Peter Micciche, who said his administration was misled about the project's scope, and Assembly Member Lenora Niesen; Rainmaker CEO Augustus Doricko conceded the company "could have done more public outreach" ([Alaska Public Media, Oct 22](https://alaskapublic.org/news/environment/2026-10-22/kenai-peninsula-borough-adopts-new-rules-for-weather-modification); [KDLL](https://www.kdll.org/local-news/2026-10-21/assembly-enacts-weather-modification-permit-process)). State Rep. Sarah Vance (R-Homer) sent a cease-and-desist letter and pledged a state bill if re-elected on November 3 ([Anchorage Daily News](https://www.adn.com/politics/alaska-legislature/2026/09/25/cloud-seeding-effort-sparks-debate-in-alaska-house-race-on-kenai-peninsula/)). Rainmaker's own rainfall figures are internally inconsistent across outlets — "19 million gallons," "about one-hundredth of an inch," and a NOAA framing of more than an inch over a square mile — which is itself a reportable accountability point about unverified self-reporting ([ADN](https://www.adn.com/politics/alaska-legislature/2026/09/25/cloud-seeding-effort-sparks-debate-in-alaska-house-race-on-kenai-peninsula/)). This is a disclosure story, not a harm story, and that is why it travels: it was passed by a cross-partisan local body in direct response to a specific operation, without relying on any contested health claim.

The statehouse year was mostly attrition. GeoLawWatch's tally for the 2026 sessions is **89 bills across 31 states plus Congress: 66 died, 8 adjourned sine die, 11 remain live, 1 was vetoed, and 1 statute was enacted** ([GeoLawWatch](https://www.geolawwatch.com/)). The enactment was Louisiana's Act 601, a one-line amendment adding "including by the burning of fuel in an aircraft engine" to the 2025 ban, which passed 29–2 and 74–22 and became law without Governor Landry's signature on June 1, 2026 — a notable non-endorsement ([LA SB 189](https://legis.la.gov/legis/BillInfo.aspx?s=26RS&b=SB189&sbi=y)). GeoLawWatch characterizes it as declaratory rather than substantive ([GeoLawWatch LA SB189](https://www.geolawwatch.com/bill/2125167)). The veto was Arizona's HB 2042, which cleared both chambers before Governor Hobbs killed it on March 13 with language the movement will hear again in every blue-governor state: "Without the science to back up its claims, HB 2042 is a so-called solution to a non-existent problem" ([Gov. Hobbs](https://azgovernor.gov/office-arizona-governor/news/2026/03/governor-katie-hobbs-legislative-action-update-0)). The near-misses are instructive: Iowa's HF 2640 passed the House 59–26 on a party-line vote and then died at the second funnel; Kentucky's HB 60 cleared its first committee and was recommitted to Judiciary; Missouri's HB 2656 got an 8–3 committee vote three days before adjournment; Mississippi SB 2418 and Alabama HB25 were reported from committee and never reached a floor ([Iowa Capital Dispatch](https://iowacapitaldispatch.com/2026/03/05/house-passes-bills-to-ban-cloud-seeding-and-to-enhance-animal-torture-penalties/); [KY HB 60](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/record/26rs/hb60.html); [GeoLawWatch MO HB2656](https://www.geolawwatch.com/bill/2065142)). Rhode Island's hearing is worth a paragraph on the site because it shows the opposition: Rainmaker testified against H 7422, and the state environmental agency told legislators it is "unaware of any such activity" in Rhode Island ([Rainmaker testimony](https://www.rilegislature.gov/Special/comdoc/House%20Environment%20and%20Natural%20Resources%202026/02-26-2026--H7422--Grayson_Gee(Rainmaker%20Technology%20Corporation)--enr.pdf); [RI DEM](https://www.rilegislature.gov/Special/comdoc/House%20Environment%20and%20Natural%20Resources%202026/02-26-2026--H7422--Terrence_Gray%20Director(RI%20DEM)--enr.pdf)). No lawsuit has challenged any enacted ban, and no state halted a cloud-seeding program; Texas's post-flood pause was lifted and its advisory board voted on August 27, 2026 only to *propose* quarterly operation reports to TDLR ([Texas Dispatch](https://thetexasdispatch.com/tdlr-weather-modification-advisory-board-august-27-2026-operation-reports-rule/)).

Two accountability findings belong on the legislation page beside the GAO numbers. First, the **GAO report's three recommendations are all still open** eight months later, NOAA has published nothing in response, and the only congressional follow-up was a May 22, 2026 letter from House Science Chairman Babin and Subcommittee Chairman McCormick asking NOAA for a majority-staff briefing — a letter E&E reported two days earlier had been circulated as a draft the committee said it "never formally sent" ([GAO tracker](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108013); [Babin/McCormick letter](https://republicans-science.house.gov/_cache/files/0/9/0933efe7-129f-4e61-a239-f50871516c1f/177E45E85678A21A928DA3AE95F5893AB1E0A1558E9431EF5251B22DDBE46247.05.22.26-iosst-to-noaa-cloud-brightening-.pdf); [E&E, May 20](https://www.eenews.net/articles/gop-lawmakers-question-federal-oversight-of-sunlight-blocking-tech/)). Second, GAO found that NOAA "makes no effort to find unreported experiments," that its forms are "virtually unchanged since 1974," and that in 2024 agencies in nine states spent at least $20 million on cloud seeding — numbers that give SkyLedger a concrete federal ask (a navigable public database, modern forms, direct notification of state agencies) rather than a vague complaint ([GAO full report](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108013/index.html); [Grist](https://grist.org/regulation/geoengineering-noaa-cloud-seeding-gao-oversight/)).

## Mainstream institutions moved toward "moratorium plus research" without the US

Readers who follow only US news will have missed that 2026 is the year international bodies adopted language the non-use movement has sought for years. On **April 21, 2026 the Council of the EU** — all 27 member states — stated that SRM poses "significant risks for the climate, the environment, security, and geopolitics" and called for "a moratorium on deployment of SRM technologies" alongside full application of the precautionary principle ([Council 8417/26](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-8417-2026-INIT/en/pdf); [Climate Home News](https://www.climatechangenews.com/2026/05/08/eu-warns-on-solar-geoengineering-but-research-debate-grinds-on/)). Three weeks later **UNEP published a working paper titled "Scientific evidence does not support SRM as a viable climate solution"** ([UNEP](https://wedocs.unep.org/items/4154b06d-6a7a-4df0-aaa9-7472e4f7c6f5)); non-use advocates objected that it still leaves room for outdoor experiments, and one researcher noted the subtitle conflates "not viable" with "evidence does not support viability," so SkyLedger should quote the title precisely rather than paraphrase it as a UN ban ([SRM360](https://srm360.org/news-reaction/unep-report-draws-mixed-reactions-from-experts/)). The G7 science academies' May 20 Arctic statement proposed a moratorium on Arctic geoengineering, though Leopoldina's Thomas Stocker called the wording "more of a compromise than an 'action document'" (the exact text could not be retrieved; verify against the [PDF](https://academie-sciences.fr/pdf/rapport/G7_Arctic_EN.pdf)) ([Royal Society](https://royalsociety.org/news/2026/05/s7-statements/)). The UK government told petitioners on April 16 that it "is not in favour of deploying Solar Radiation Modification and has no plans to do so" ([UK Parliament petition 758008](https://petition.parliament.uk/petitions/758008)). India's NITI Aayog became the first Asian government body to announce plans to regulate solar geoengineering, citing monsoon and crop risks — a single-source item from ABC Australia ([ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-06-23/solar-geoengineering-srm-private-companies-monsoon-india/106805998)). In October, London Protocol parties adopted lab-first assessment frameworks for ocean alkalinity enhancement and biomass sinking, while the 2013 marine-geoengineering amendment remains stuck at 9 of the 37 ratifications needed ([Columbia Climate Law](https://blogs.law.columbia.edu/climatechange/2026/10/07/parties-to-the-london-convention-and-protocol-debate-marine-carbon-dioxide-removal-governance/); [IEAGHG](https://ieaghg.org/news/london-convention-and-london-protocol-annual-meeting-2025/)). Two cautions: SRM was never on UNEA-7's formal agenda, so no UN decision exists to cite ([DSG](https://www.sgdeliberation.org/dsg-writing/unea-7-and-the-quiet-conversation-on-srm)), and Mexico's much-cited 2023 "ban" remains an administrative announcement whose enactment SRM360 calls "unclear" ([SRM360 tracker](https://srm360.org/article/srm-bans-and-moratoria/)).

The EU paired restraint with surveillance money: an October 6, 2026 Horizon Europe tender of **€35 million for detecting, monitoring and attributing SRM activities**, explicitly excluding field experiments ([Cyprus funding portal](https://www.fundingprogrammesportal.gov.cy/en/call/detection-monitoring-modelling-and-attribution-capabilities-for-solar-radiation-modification-srm-activities-and-assessing-their-impacts-on-earths-climate-and-ecosystems-en-2026/); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/europe/article/3369959/eu-wants-track-potential-rogue-efforts-block-out-sun)). That matches the year's most useful peer-reviewed result for a transparency site: Smith, Alberola, Boers et al. found that large-scale covert stratospheric aerosol injection would likely be detected by existing satellite and aircraft monitoring ([Environmental Research: Climate](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2752-5295/ae7148)). Other 2026 science worth a sentence each: SAI could substantially alter the Indian summer monsoon ([Wilson & Bala](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2752-5295/ae7599)); high-latitude SAI would expose airline passengers to sulfate ([Robock et al., GRL](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2026GL122804)); and four senior climate scientists — Pierrehumbert, Slingo, Mann and Masson-Delmotte — wrote in the Guardian on June 19 that deployment would have to continue "for a century or more," that models disagree on cooling by a factor of three, and that it is proceeding in "the total absence of governance" (Guardian text not directly fetched; summarized via [CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/06/21/termination-shock-the-existential-danger-of-geoengineering/)). MIT Technology Review's two-part June "reality check" found no existing aircraft can carry the required loads to altitude and that aerosol chemistry remains unsettled ([Part 1](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/06/17/1138743/hacking-atmosphere-geoengineering-reality-check/); [Part 2](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/06/18/1139227/geoengineering-engineering-challenges/)). No NASEM, AGU, AMS or Royal Society issued a new SRM statement in 2026, so the site should not claim a new consensus statement exists.

On the ground, the only ARIA-funded outdoor experiment that has actually run is Arctic sea-ice re-thickening in Cambridge Bay, Nunavut, which reported treated ice up to ~50 cm thicker by late May (company-linked figures, no peer review yet); ARIA says "no locations have yet been confirmed for any outdoor experiments in the UK," and the UK marine cloud brightening project is not scheduled before 2028 ([ARIA one-year update](https://aria.org.uk/insights/exploring-climate-cooling-one-year-in); [ARIA FAQ](https://aria.org.uk/opportunity-spaces/resilient-climate-and-ecosystems/exploring-climate-cooling/faqs); [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/real-ice-cambridge-bay-nunavut/)). Any site language implying ARIA "spraying flights" have occurred is unsupported. The University of Washington's cloud-brightening program remains halted since Alameda in 2024 ([Seattle Times](https://www.seattletimes.com/nation-world/uw-cloud-brightening-study-in-california-is-halted-by-local-officials/)). Cloud seeding expanded abroad: the UAE logged 110 missions by August; Delhi's trials returned "mixed results" ([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/abnews/uae-cloud-seeding-operations-2026); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/24/cloud-seeding-climate-water-demand-rain-snow.html)). No 2026 flood anywhere produced an official finding implicating cloud seeding.

## Washington stalled while Republicans split three ways

Nothing federal passed, was marked up, or received a hearing in 2026. The durable facts are the GAO report, two new House bills stuck at referral, one staff-briefing letter, and the loss of the Clear Skies Act's sponsor. What changed is the ideology. A pro-SRM Republican argument appeared in March: within weeks, the Rainey Center (with SilverLining "in collaboration") published "Protecting American Weather Sovereignty," ACCF's George David Banks — a first-term Trump energy adviser — argued US leadership on SRM is "a national security imperative," and Texas's TCCRI issued "Clearing the Air"; all three oppose bans and frame SRM as a China-race question, and none disclosed funders ([Rainey brief](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/663a60b91deeae06e8ae1d1c/69a9a377787b2574dca51bde_RaineyCenter_ProtectingAmericanWeatherSovereignty_Brief_8.5x11_r5_digital%20(1).pdf); [ACCF](https://accf.org/new-report-u-s-leadership-on-solar-radiation-management-is-a-national-security-imperative/); [SRM360, Apr 3](https://srm360.org/news-article/do-new-reports-signal-shift-in-us-conservatives/)). In July Banks told an Aspen Institute panel SRM would be "a good insurance policy for continued fossil fuel use" ([E&E, July 23](https://www.eenews.net/articles/trump-ally-floats-republican-pitch-for-sun-blocking-technologies/)). On the other flank, **The GeoFight**, a conservative group launched in February 2026 and led by attorneys who dispute mainstream climate science, is fundraising to sue SRM startups, launched a "Stop Stardust" petition on May 22, and promotes *Mabie v. United States* (No. 1:26-cv-00274-SBP, Denver), a weather-data-manipulation suit covered only by the group and affiliated outlets ([E&E, Aug 3](https://www.eenews.net/articles/how-a-conspiracy-fueled-campaign-could-turn-republicans-against-weather-modification/); [GeoFight release](https://thegeofight.substack.com/p/for-immediate-release-first-of-its)). ## Arkansas would be a first-time filer with three models and one veto to study

No Arkansas geoengineering or weather-modification bill, resolution, interim study, or legislator champion exists in any tracker, outlet, or the arkleg.state.ar.us domain; Arkansas is absent from GeoLawWatch's 31-state 2026 list, making it one of roughly 13 states that have never filed ([GeoLawWatch](https://www.geolawwatch.com/)). That absence is not evidence of opposition — the 2026 fiscal session was appropriations-only — but it means the January bill will have no in-state precedent, co-sponsor history, or committee familiarity to build on. The calendar: **pre-filing opens November 16, 2026; the 2027 Regular Session convenes January 11, 2027 and is scheduled to end April 28** ([Arkansas Legislature 2027 dates](https://www.arkleg.state.ar.us/Home/FTPDocument?path=%2Fassembly%2F2027%2FDocuments%2FSession_Important_Dates_2027.pdf)). The one Arkansas-specific claim in circulation — chemtrail.app's line that the state "narrowly avoided a cloud seeding ban in February 2025" — is unsourced and uncorroborated; do not use it ([chemtrail.app](https://chemtrail.app/states)). Reclaim Our Skies is circulating model legislation and an open letter for 2027 sessions ([Reclaim Our Skies toolkit](https://www.reclaimourskies.com/portfolio-collections/tool-kit/legislation-to-prohibit-geoengineering-weather-modification)).

The three enacted laws offer three distinct drafting templates, and the choice matters more than the toolkit scripts currently imply. **Tennessee's** is a bare one-sentence prohibition with no penalty, definitions or enforcement — simplest to pass, hardest to enforce ([SA0653](https://www.capitol.tn.gov/Bills/113/Amend/SA0653.pdf)). **Louisiana's** is a reporting-and-repeal model: it put the prohibition inside the Environmental Quality Act, directed LDEQ to take citizen reports and publish them, carved out firefighting, agriculture and forestry below 1,000 feet, and repealed the dormant licensing statute; the House stripped penalties to get it through ([Act 95 digest](https://legis.la.gov/LEGIS/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1426146)). **Florida's** is the felony model — up to five years and $100,000 — paired with a monthly airport-reporting duty to FDOT that the Attorney General has actively enforced ([Florida Senate](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2025/56); [CBS12](https://cbs12.com/news/local/florida-ag-enforces-new-law-banning-weather-modification-at-public-airports-james-uthmeier-altering-temperature-weather-climate-or-sunlight-intensity-south-florida-news-july-14-2025)). Two threshold questions the notes could not answer and that must be settled before drafting: whether Arkansas has an existing weather-modification licensing statute to repeal (Tennessee, Florida and Louisiana all did), and which agency — the Division of Environmental Quality or the Natural Resources Commission — would take reports. Florida's DEP records showed no licence application since 1957 ([PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/article/2025/jul/21/florida-weather-modification-airports-geoengineeri/)); an equivalent Arkansas records check would pre-empt the "non-existent problem" line.

The 2026 failures are a syllabus. Arizona died on a Democratic governor's desk with a quote about conspiracy theories; Arkansas's governor is Republican, so the veto risk differs, but the "non-existent problem" framing will arrive through agency testimony instead — Rhode Island's environmental director told legislators his agency was "unaware of any such activity," and Rainmaker now sends a witness to hearings ([RI DEM](https://www.rilegislature.gov/Special/comdoc/House%20Environment%20and%20Natural%20Resources%202026/02-26-2026--H7422--Terrence_Gray%20Director(RI%20DEM)--enr.pdf); [Rainmaker testimony](https://www.rilegislature.gov/Special/comdoc/House%20Environment%20and%20Natural%20Resources%202026/02-26-2026--H7422--Grayson_Gee(Rainmaker%20Technology%20Corporation)--enr.pdf)). Iowa passed a chamber and still died on a procedural funnel deadline; Missouri cleared committee three days before adjournment. Kentucky's recommittal to Judiciary shows how a felony penalty invites a second committee. The counter-arguments have also matured: FAS ("conspiracy theories are not a good reason to ban geoengineering research") and the University of Houston Law Center ("The Case Against Blanket Bans," which also supplies the useful figure of three outright bans among 133 bills in 38 states since 2023) are now the standard academic rebuttals, and the Rainey/ACCF "weather sovereignty" and China-race framing gives Republican opponents a conservative reason to vote no ([FAS](https://fas.org/publication/conspiracy-theories-are-not-a-good-reason-to-ban-geoengineering-research/); [UH Law](https://law.uh.edu/news/summer2026/0720EENR.asp)). Louisiana's new "burning of fuel in an aircraft engine" language is the kind of clause that hands opponents the "chemtrails" label; GeoLawWatch itself calls it declaratory ([GeoLawWatch LA SB189](https://www.geolawwatch.com/bill/2125167)).

The strongest Arkansas-specific case is built from what the research shows is working: the GAO finding that NOAA does not notify state agencies and that four of ten states had operations missing from the federal database, which justifies a state-level reporting duty on its own terms; the Kenai ordinance, which shows a disclosure regime (chemicals, quantities, flight logs, intended outcome) passing a Republican-led body in weeks; the Alaska permitting vacuum, where two state agencies each said the operation was not theirs to permit; and Texas's August 2026 move toward quarterly operator reports, which shows even a pro-seeding state tightening disclosure ([GAO full report](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108013/index.html); [KDLL](https://www.kdll.org/local-news/2026-10-21/assembly-enacts-weather-modification-permit-process); [Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/news/2026-09-10/a-california-based-company-made-it-rain-near-homer-locals-say-they-were-blindsided); [Texas Dispatch](https://thetexasdispatch.com/tdlr-weather-modification-advisory-board-august-27-2026-operation-reports-rule/)). Neighboring Tennessee and Louisiana supply the regional precedent; Texas, Oklahoma, Missouri and Mississippi supply the cautionary list of neighbors whose bills died. The toolkit scripts should stop citing "30+ states with bills pending," "bipartisan landslides," and Stardust's April experiments, and start citing the GAO numbers correctly, the 650+ academic signatories, the Kenai ordinance, and the EU and UNEP positions — all of which survive hostile fact-checking.

## Conclusion

The audit's most important finding is not any single error but a pattern: the site's loudest pages were written at a moment of maximum alarm (spring 2026) about events that then did not happen, while the real 2026 developments — a company retreating under scrutiny and publishing its chemistry, a borough writing disclosure rules in two months, 27 EU governments calling for a deployment moratorium, a federal auditor documenting that nobody is tracking these operations — are stronger material for a transparency platform than the predictions were. A site that updates visibly ("we said April; here is what happened instead, with the patents") earns more trust than one that keeps an URGENT banner for an experiment that never flew. The correction table is the price of that trust, and items 1 through 7 should ship before any Arkansas legislator is pointed at the site.

For the January bill, the research suggests the movement's advantage has shifted from prohibition to disclosure. Every ban that passed in 2025–26 passed either without penalties or with a reporting duty attached, every ban that died in 2026 died on procedure or on a "non-existent problem" argument, and the one measure adopted in direct response to an actual operation was a permit-and-disclose ordinance from a Republican mayor. An Arkansas bill drafted around GAO's own recommendations — mandatory notification of the state agency, public posting of what is dispersed, where, and by whom — with a prohibition on stratospheric aerosol release layered on top would be harder to caricature, easier to defend in committee, and more consistent with what SkyLedger already is: a ledger.
